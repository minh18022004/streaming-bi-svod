# Written Insights — Prime Video Content Strategy

## 1. Content identity: the "everything store" of streaming

The platform with the largest catalog within the dataset is Prime Video, which offers 5,944 titles (4,113 movies, 1,831 TV shows). Netflix comes second with 5,666 titles (3,756 movies, 1,910 TV shows). Hulu has 2,668 titles (1,648 movies, 1,020 TV shows) while Disney+ has the fewest at 1,273 titles (956 movies, 317 TV shows). Furthermore, Prime Video's catalog also includes titles that were released as far back as the 1950s, whereas the majority of Netflix’s content was released after 2015. Finally, Prime Video has 69% movies in its catalog which is the highest percentage of movies of all of the platforms in the dataset.

Interpretation: As the “everything store” of streaming, Prime Video mirrors Amazon’s retail strategy. While the company’s focus on offering a variety of content allows Prime Video to acquire subscribers based on the idea that there is something for everyone, it makes it harder for customers to discover new content and weakens the brand association with specific types of content.

## 2. The quality perception gap

Prime Video boasts the lowest average Rotten Tomatoes scores of the four platforms, with around 50 for movies and 38 for TV shows - Netflix scores around 54 in each category, Hulu scores 60 for movies and 53 for TV shows, and Disney+ scores 58 for movies and 49 for TV shows. The box plots illustrate the number of low-scoring titles on the streaming service.

Overall, the strategy of aiming for a broad range of content comes at a cost to the average quality of the library. Prime Video has opted to focus instead on its popular titles and genres such as limited edition movies like Fallout, Reacher, The Boys, and The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power, as well as live sports broadcasts.

## 3. Market context: attention is shifting, and sports moves Prime's needle

Public market data frames the opportunity:

- Streaming services pulled in 47.5% of all TV viewing in the U.S. in December 2025 (Nielsen, The Gauge), outshining both the broadcast and cable networks individually.

- Prime Video garnered 4.3% of all U.S. TV viewing in December - a 12% increase from the previous month - thanks to the addition of NFL Thursday Night Football games and the new season of Fallout. Nevertheless, the streaming service trails behind its closest competitors with YouTube at 12.7% and Netflix at 9.0%.

- Prime Video also boasts the largest audience for platforms featuring advertisements. It drew in 315 million advertising viewers worldwide - up from 200 million in April 2024 - with over 130 million of those viewers in the United States. This compares to Netflix with 190 million monthly active viewers using their advertising channel.

While Prime Video’s viewership depends on specific events, the advertisement revenue from both its Prime Video channel and its $2.99-per-month advertising-free channel encourage viewers to stream its content regularly to benefit from the available subscription plan. Prime Video can increase its revenue by narrowing the viewing gap between its channel and that of its competitors.

## 4. SWOT summary

| | |
|---|---|
| **Strengths** | Largest catalog; largest premium ad audience at 315M; Prime-bundle customers stay with Amazon; rights to several of the most popular sports franchises |\
| **Weaknesses** | Only 4.3% of U.S. television time; the lowest average content scores for all streaming services; viewers primarily watch for special events; no metric for number of subscribers |\
| **Opportunities** | Percentage of viewers watching streaming services at 50%; the first full seasons of NBA and WNBA; $7-10 billion in estimated advertising revenue for Amazon; ad-free viewing plans to generate additional revenue |\
| **Threats** | Competition from YouTube, which has the largest lead in the industry; Netflix competes for advertising budgets with its own advertising plan; increasing costs of popular sports channels; subscribers are saturated with various streaming services

## 5. Strategic recommendation and expected outcome

Recommendation: Make the NBA/WNBA season a weekly appointment to NBA and WNBA networks, as well as to the catalog of sports documentaries and documentaries by the players themselves - transforming event viewers into habitual, weekly viewers to be monetized through advertising.

Impact: A weekly appointment to sports networks will lead to an increase in the number of viewers of those networks and the number of viewing hours of those viewers. An increase in the number of viewers and viewing hours will lead to an increase in the number of advertisements viewed by those viewers (ad impressions), as well as an increase in the number of viewers who opt for ad-free viewing subscriptions. Thus, there will be an increase in the revenue from both advertisement and subscription sales to Prime Video.

Estimate: At the current 130 million U.S. monthly viewers of Prime Video that feature ads, a 10% increase in viewers (an increase from December 2025 when sports channels drew a 12% increase in viewers) will increase the number of viewers by 13 million viewers. If the 6.5 million engaged with sports on Prime Video channels opt for the $2.99 monthly ad-free tier (which amounts to $35.88 per year for each subscriber), that alone will bring in around $233 million in additional revenue for Prime Video each year. Furthermore, there will also be additional revenue from the additional ads impressions from those additional viewers and their viewing hours; while advertising revenue from ads on Prime Video is currently around $7 billion in annual revenue (with industry analysts projecting it to reach between $10 billion and $7 billion per year), such ad revenue is likely to grow with the increasing popularity of sports on the channel. Finally, the habit of viewers to watch sports on Prime Video will lead to the retention of the Prime members that view the videos on the service.
